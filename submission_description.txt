This report presents an exploratory data analysis and text-preprocessing workflow for the Cornell sentence polarity dataset v1.0, associated with Pang and Lee and distributed through Cornell University. The corpus consists of short English-language Rotten Tomatoes movie-review snippets. It contains 10,662 labeled examples, with 5,331 positive and 5,331 negative snippets. The report distinguishes this sentence-level dataset from Cornell's separate document-level polarity dataset v2.0 and describes the source, label construction, unit of analysis, and limitations of deriving each sentence label from an overall Fresh or Rotten review rating.

The EDA examines sentiment-label distribution, character and token lengths, per-snippet lexical diversity, total vocabulary, frequent unigrams, adjacent bigrams, and simple language cues such as negation and punctuation. The measured corpus contains 201,517 tokens and 18,989 distinct token types under the documented regular-expression tokenizer. The average snippet contains 18.90 tokens, the median is 18, and the middle 50 percent range from 12 to 25 tokens. The labels are exactly balanced at 50 percent per class. Quality checks found no blank rows and no exact duplicates in either raw or normalized text. Four snippets contained markup-like tags; no URL-like or email-like strings were found, while digits and punctuation were retained for inspection rather than deleted without justification.

The preprocessing workflow preserves each original snippet and creates a separate normalized text field. It lowercases text, replaces URL and email patterns when present, removes markup syntax, removes most non-linguistic symbols, and standardizes whitespace. Tokenization preserves simple contractions and numbers. Negation terms such as no and not are deliberately retained because they can reverse sentiment. Stopword filtering is limited to selected frequency summaries and is not applied destructively to the cleaned text.

The report includes statistical tables, class-wise comparisons, visualizations, preprocessing rationale, data-quality findings, challenges, and recommendations for future NLP modeling. A Python script is supplied to retrieve the public corpus, recreate the analysis, and export CSV/JSON summaries and charts. The analysis is descriptive and does not claim a trained classifier or model accuracy. Future work should establish a TF-IDF baseline, use stratified evaluation, prevent vectorizer leakage, and report precision, recall, F1-score, and confusion-matrix results.
